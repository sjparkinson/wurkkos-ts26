# Hazard log

One row for every way identified so far that this torch could hurt someone or itself, what covers it, and what evidence there is that the cover works. It's a log of the current state. A row changes in the same commit as the code it describes, and a hazard nobody has thought of isn't in here, so a short table doesn't mean a short list.

Nothing here has met a real TS26. The [roadmap](roadmap.md) says the same for section 2, and [breakout.md](breakout.md) is the plan that would change it. So read the Status column strictly.

- **code only** means the code does it and nothing has run it, not even on the simulator.
- **simulator** means the firmware has run it on the fake chip (`cargo xtask hex`), or a host test checks the library. The simulator is one reading of the datasheet, run against code written from that same reading, so a pass finds mistakes and proves nothing. [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#the-simulator) lists what it leaves out.
- **bench** means it's been seen working on a real TS26.
- **measured** means a number from a real TS26 backs the constant.

Nothing is past simulator today. Some rows also cite a run on the breakout's chip, which is an ATtiny1616 but not a torch, and none of those moves a row.

| H1. The boost converter on with nothing controlling it | PB2 (the enable) is a floating input from reset until firmware drives it, or is left high after a stray jump to the reset vector, or is raised before the duty is set | `hw::hold_enable_low` is the first call in `main`, and writes OUT before DIR. `hw::set_output` sets the duty and then raises the enable, and drops the enable and then zeroes the duty. `hw::halt` (also the panic handler) goes through the same off order. `hw::init` never gives PB2 a pull-up | Simulator `found_lit` starts with PORTB dirty. The simulator turns PB2 high with no duty into an error, and so a PB2 that stays high with the duty at 0 for longer than the delta-sigma's longest gap. Tests `the_enable_goes_after_the_duty`, `a_dsm_gap_is_fine_but_a_longer_zero_duty_is_not`, `the_dangerous_pins_never_get_a_pull_up`, `power_down_wants_pb2_low` and `found_lit_wants_out_cleared_by_the_first_register_write` in xtask/src/sim.rs. | H2. A hang with the light on | The loop stops, so PB2 stays high and the PWM carries on | `hw::start_watchdog` sets a 32-clock window and `watchdog::Feeder` says which passes feed it. A tick that stops while the light's on has its own deadline, `watchdog::TICK_DEADLINE_CYCLES` (2048 PWM cycles, about 104 ms), after which `main` calls `hw::halt`. The next boot after a bite is capped (`selftest::hung`) | Simulator `slow_ticks` runs every tick as slow as the datasheet allows, and `oscillator_stops` stops the 32 kHz oscillator with the light on. Tests `every_feed_lands_mid_window` and `the_tick_deadline_is_104_ms_against_ticks_of_306_cycles_or_460_at_worst` in src/watchdog.rs, and `a_feed_in_the_open_window_is_fine_and_a_closed_one_bites` and `a_stopped_oscillator_stops_the_pit_and_the_watchdog` in xtask/src/sim.rs | simulator | Nobody has seen the watchdog bite. The simulator has no resets, so a bite is a failed run and what follows one never runs. The watchdog and the tick share an oscillator, which is why the deadline counts PWM cycles off the other one. [breakout.md](breakout.md) T4 is still open, so `main` idles on the tick after a feed and costs about 0.37 mA at 3 V, or about 0.6 mA at 4.2 V (a third of the typical idle current in DS40002204A table 36-4, interpolated between its 3 V and 5 V rows). A dead 32 kHz oscillator with the light off leaves the chip asleep with PB2 low, which is probably the safe state and nobody has seen |
| H3. Over-discharge of an unprotected 21700 | The Wurkkos cell has no protection of its own, and the MP3432 pulls more current as the cell falls | `battery::Protection` steps the ceiling down below `battery::STEP_DOWN_BELOW` (3.00 V) and fades it down and off below `battery::OFF_BELOW` (2.90 V). A rest keeps the ceiling (`Protection::rest`). `battery::BLIND_SAMPLES` covers a cell it can't read (H12) | `a_low_cell_steps_down_gradually_then_turns_off` and `a_flat_cell_fades_down_the_ramp_before_it_turns_off` in src/limits.rs. In src/battery.rs, `steps_down_after_5_seconds_below_3_volts`, `a_second_below_2_9_volts_drops_a_big_step_rather_than_turning_off`, `turns_off_after_a_second_below_2_9_volts_at_level_1`, `a_long_rest_keeps_the_ceiling_and_forgets_the_filter`, and `every_reading_held_ends_where_the_thresholds_say`, which holds every reading the ADC can give. docs/ui.md's `cell:2.95V` and `cell:2.80V` rows, run by the simulator | simulator | Every threshold is unmeasured. `battery::SLOPE` (1094) is the stock firmware's figure for another sample and hasn't been checked against a multimeter (7.2). The 2.7 V the MP3432 wants to start leaves little over the 2.90 V cut-off (7.3). Charging the cell down on purpose hasn't been done (7.1) |
| H4. Overheating | A lit light in still air, or a head that's covered | `thermal::Thermal` steps the ceiling down towards `thermal::FLOOR` (level 50) as the smoothed and predicted temperature passes `thermal::LIMIT` (50 °C at the sensor). `ramp::CEILING` holds the light at level 120 of 150 until that's been checked on a real head | `a_hot_head_fades_down_to_the_floor` and `turning_on_resets_thermal_regulation` in src/limits.rs. The ceiling at level 120 is itself unmeasured, so nobody knows what it draws or how hot the head gets (5.6, 7.6) |
| H5. Lit in a pocket or a bag | A click on the switch under pressure | The 4C lockout (docs/ui.md). In lockout `ui::Ui` ignores clicks, and a hold lights level 13 for as long as it's held. The aux shows red | `lockout_ignores_clicks_and_allows_a_momentary_light`, `off_leaves_a_locked_torch_locked` and `aux_is_blue_when_off_red_in_lockout_and_out_with_the_light_on` in src/ui.rs. `lockout_lights_at_the_floor_only_while_held` and `every_example_in_docs_ui_md_holds` in src/torch.rs. Lockout lives in RAM, so a reset (a bite, or a cell swap) drops back to off, unlocked. Nobody's tried the switch's actual feel under pressure |
| H6. Corrupt or half-written flash | An interrupted or bad flash, or a bit that's flipped | `hw::flash_ok` runs CRCSCAN over all of flash at power-on and `selftest::Faults::flash` caps the light at `limits::FAULT_CEILING` (level 50). `crc::sign` puts a stamp of the commit just before the CRC in the last two bytes, so `backup` shows which build a torch has, and `cargo xtask hex` writes only that. `signed()` in xtask/src/main.rs makes `flash` refuse a full image whose CRC doesn't check | `the_scan_passes_a_signed_image_and_fails_a_changed_one` in xtask/src/sim.rs. In xtask/src/crc.rs, `matches_the_crc_16_ccitt_false_check_value`, `a_signed_image_fills_flash_and_ends_in_its_checksum` and `the_whole_of_a_signed_flash_checks_to_zero`. `flash_takes_only_a_signed_image` in xtask/src/main.rs. `a_failed_self_test_holds_the_fault_ceiling_for_good` in src/limits.rs. docs/ui.md's `flash:corrupt` row | simulator | The scan and the CRC convention have only met the simulator's reading of the datasheet (6.1). The check only caps the light at level 50, it doesn't stop it. A flipped bit after power-on isn't noticed until the next power-on |
| H7. Wrong fuses | A torch with a fuse the firmware's timings or its own protection don't expect | `selftest::Fuses::ok` checks `WDTCFG`, `OSCCFG` (frequency select and `OSCLOCK`), `SYSCFG0.RSTPINCFG`, `APPEND` and `BOOTEND`, with `hw::fuses` only ever reading them. A failure caps the light at level 50. Nothing in this repo writes a fuse | `factory_fuses_pass`, `checks_fields_not_whole_bytes` and `each_wrong_field_fails` in src/selftest.rs. `only_a_fault_caps_the_light` in src/selftest.rs. docs/ui.md's `fault` row, run by the simulator. The check failed on the breakout's chip, whose `SYSCFG0` reads 0xFF (0.3), and the full firmware then held at level 50, though pin 14 was grounded at the time too, so that run can't separate the two causes (0.10) | simulator | Nobody has read a TS26's fuses (1.9). A bad fuse is the one thing in the table that's permanent, which is why nothing here writes one. `WDTCFG` and the reset pin are checked and `BODCFG` isn't (1.10) |
| H8. Losing UPDI or the chip while programming | A wrong `SYSCFG0` write, 12 V on the pad, a slipped probe, or a wrong pad order | The programmer never writes fuses, the user row or a locked chip, and refuses a chip with the wrong signature. No high-voltage programmer ever goes on this torch, recovery included. The first flash on any torch is `first-light` (commit `9d3b7dc`), and the full firmware goes on only after that's proven on the light. `restore` refuses a backup from another chip | `refuses_another_chip` and `refuses_a_locked_chip` in xtask/src/updi.rs. `restore_refuses_a_backup_from_another_chip_and_writes_nothing` in xtask/src/main.rs. [flashing.md](flashing.md) and [breakout.md](breakout.md) have the rules and the order | code only | No test shows that no command path writes a fuse. It's true by reading the code, and `nvm_commands_are_the_datasheets` in xtask/src/updi.rs only pins the command numbers. The pads on one TS26 have been found and their `R - +` silkscreen read (1.3), and their pitch fits the kit's probe (1.4). Nobody has measured what they connect to (1.5 and 1.6). The breakout ran the programmer through its own steps, which isn't the torch's board |
| H9. A stray interrupt | An interrupt that something enables by mistake, or whose flag was already up, with a vector that does nothing | `__unhandled_interrupt` in src/avr/rt.rs zeroes r1 and jumps into `hw::halt`, which turns interrupts off, drops PB2, zeroes the duty and powers down. A `reti` would instead have returned with the flag still up, so the loop would crawl one instruction per interrupt with PB2 possibly high. Nothing enables any interrupt but the PIT and TCA0's overflow | The simulator makes it an error to enable an interrupt when its vector goes to `__unhandled_interrupt`. `vectors` in xtask/src/layout.rs insists the vector is `eor r1, r1` and a `jmp` to exactly `hw::halt` (`accepts_an_unhandled_interrupt_that_clears_r1_and_jumps_to_halt`, `rejects_an_unhandled_interrupt_that_doesnt_clear_r1_and_jump`). The simulator run `unhandled_interrupt` forces one on a lit torch and checks it ends dark in power-down | simulator | The forced interrupt is a one-shot stand-in. A real stray one stays enabled and flagged, and the datasheet doesn't say whether a pending interrupt that can't be taken wakes `halt`'s sleep. PB2 is low before the first sleep either way, so the worst case is the chip spinning dark until the watchdog resets it, about 63 ms later, if the 32 kHz oscillator runs. Nobody has seen the watchdog bite |
| H10. A panic path in the firmware | An overflow, an index or an unwrap in code that reaches a panic | `hw::halt` is the panic handler (`panic` in src/main.rs). It turns the light off, powers down, and leaves the watchdog to reset the chip. `cargo xtask hex` refuses an image that links any panic machinery (`no_panics` in xtask/src/layout.rs), so today nothing can reach it | `accepts_symbols_with_no_panic_machinery` and `rejects_a_panic_handler_or_a_bounds_check` in xtask/src/layout.rs. A forced out-of-bounds index in the loop made `cargo xtask hex` fail, naming `rust_begin_unwind`, `panic_bounds_check` and `panic_fmt` | simulator | The check matches symbol names, which can change with the nightly, so it has to be re-proven on every bump (CONTRIBUTING.md). src/lib.rs denies clippy's `arithmetic_side_effects`, `indexing_slicing`, `cast_possible_truncation`, `cast_sign_loss`, `cast_possible_wrap`, `as_conversions`, `unwrap_used`, `expect_used`, `panic` and `unreachable` in the library, and src/main.rs denies the same in the AVR build, which covers src/avr. So each site either saturates, checks, or carries an `#[expect]` saying why it can't overflow. Release builds have `overflow-checks = true` (Cargo.toml), so any `+`, `-`, `*` (compound forms included), negation or shift by too many bits that LLVM can't prove fits links a panic and `no_panics` fails the build. That machine-checks, for the whole firmware and not only the library, that none of those wraps unless it says `wrapping_*`. Bits shifted out of the top (`centivolts << 4` in `battery::Protection::filter`, for one) are never checked, and nor is a truncating `as` cast. Casts rest on the clippy denies above, and lost shift bits on reading. An unprovable `u8 + 1` added to `gradual::Gradual::tick` made `cargo xtask hex` fail, naming `rust_begin_unwind`, `panic_const_add_overflow` and `panic_fmt`. It proves no wrap, and nothing more. A value that saturates or clamps where it shouldn't still passes, and the `#[expect]` reasons for why those never trigger are read, not machine-checked |
| H11. A stuck switch | Dirt, a pressed-in switch, or a jammed button | The UI's holds are bounded. From off, a 1H lights level 13 and nothing more happens however long it's held (`ui::Ui` swallows the hold). With the light on, a held switch ramps up and stops at `ramp::CEILING`, and both ceilings (H3, H4) still apply to the level it asks for | `hold_from_off_is_the_floor_and_does_not_ramp`, `ramps_stop_at_the_ends` and `a_hold_that_outlasts_off_does_not_light_it_again` in src/ui.rs. `the_ramp_never_asks_for_more_than_the_ceiling`, `a_switch_held_from_power_on_stays_at_the_floor` and `a_switch_held_while_on_still_answers_to_heat` in src/torch.rs. The `1H:3s` rows of docs/ui.md, and `switch_stuck` (twenty thousand ticks held from power-on), run by the simulator | simulator | A stuck switch keeps the light on at the level it reached, because nothing times it out (H5). Nobody has measured how short a press the switch gives, or how it bounces |
| H12. A dead or garbage ADC | A broken ADC, a shorted or open PA1, or a reading from a rest | `battery::plausible` (1.00 to 4.40 V) and `thermal::plausible` reject readings that can't be real. A few are let go and then each side lowers its own ceiling, the battery side to `limits::FAULT_CEILING` and the thermal side to its floor. With the light on, `battery::BLIND_SAMPLES` (five minutes) without one reading it can believe turns it off, and a click brings it back, still capped, for another five minutes | `a_dead_adc_brings_both_ceilings_down`, `no_result_at_all_is_garbage_too`, `garbage_voltages_cap_it_at_the_fault_ceiling_then_turn_it_off_at_five_minutes`, `a_garbage_temperature_alone_never_turns_it_off` and `garbage_from_power_on_shows_nothing_from_off` in src/limits.rs. In src/battery.rs, `implausible_samples_never_reach_the_filter`, `a_second_of_garbage_drops_to_the_fault_ceiling` and `five_minutes_of_garbage_turns_it_off_and_not_a_sample_sooner`. In src/thermal.rs, `single_glitches_are_ignored`, `a_jump_that_lasts_is_believed` and `a_broken_sensor_steps_down_to_the_floor`. `a_result_with_no_conversion_started_is_ignored` in src/sensors.rs. docs/ui.md's `cell:0V` rows, and `adc_never_answers` (an ADC that never finishes a conversion), run by the simulator. The breakout ran the can't-read-the-cell path on a real chip with PA1 grounded, and it went off at five minutes (0.10) | simulator | A reading that's plausible and frozen isn't detected, so a stuck ADC that shows a good voltage looks like a good cell. A garbage temperature never turns the light off, only holds it at the floor, which is a choice and unmeasured. Five minutes is a judgement. `a_plausible_voltage_that_never_changes_keeps_the_light_on` and `a_plausible_temperature_that_never_changes_never_steps_down` in src/limits.rs pin that gap down rather than close it |
| H13. A wrong clock | A wrong `OSCCFG` frequency select, a locked oscillator calibration, or a prescaler that isn't the one assumed | `selftest::Fuses::ok` checks `FREQSEL` and `OSCLOCK`. The prescaler is the firmware's own write in `hw::init` (`CLKCTRL_MCLKCTRLB`, divide by 2), and the simulator checks the clock it ends up with (`clocked_as_modelled` in xtask/src/sim.rs). `watchdog::CLOCK_HZ` is that clock, and the tick deadline's sums start from it. On the breakout, `first-light`'s PWM read 19.79 to 19.80 kHz against 19.61 expected, so about 0.9 % fast (0.6) | `each_wrong_field_fails` in src/selftest.rs and `clocked_as_modelled`. The breakout's frequency reading, which is first-light on a different chip | simulator | A power-on clock check was considered and left out. DS40002204A tables 36-12 and 36-13 give OSC20M as ±5 % and OSCULP32K as ±30 % over the full operating range, characterisation and not production tested. Their ratio can be +50 % or −27 % on a good chip, so a window that never fails a good part only catches a clock that's doubled or halved, which the fuse check and the simulator already cover. A dead OSC20M stops the CPU, and a dead OSCULP32K is H2's deadline |

## What's left

This is true of every row, so it's kept out of the table.

- **Every threshold is unmeasured.** The cell's cut-offs, the temperature limit, the level 120 ceiling, the five minutes, the 104 ms and the fault ceiling are all from the stock firmware, the datasheet or the project's own judgement. None of them has been checked on a TS26.
- **The simulator's limits** are in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#the-simulator). The two that matter most here are that it has no chip resets, so a watchdog bite and the boot after it never run, and that it models nothing analogue, so the driver, the cell and the head are all scripted.
- **Frozen but plausible readings** get past both `battery::plausible` and `thermal::plausible`. Nothing in the firmware can tell a stuck reading from a steady one, and two tests in src/limits.rs pin that down so a change to it shows.
- **There's no run-time limit.** A lit light with a good cell and a cool head stays lit, whatever pressed the switch.
- **[breakout.md](breakout.md) is what would move rows to bench.** Stage 3's `first-light` run is the gate for H1 and H8, stage 6 for H2, H6 and H7, and stage 7 for H3, H4 and H12's thresholds. Until a row has a result there, it stays where it is.
